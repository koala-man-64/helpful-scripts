## Execution incidents and lessons

1. **A safe queue is an executable fallback, not a watch list.** Yahoo selected
   Waddle before the planned retarget completed. The player was useful, but the
   control outcome was unintended. The top queue entry must match the current
   acceptable emergency pick before the clock becomes short.
2. **A timed-out click has an unknown outcome.** The Zay Flowers action timed
   out even though Yahoo accepted it. Re-reading last pick, roster, queue, and
   turn state prevented a duplicate action.
3. **Batch queue maintenance races the room.** Several targets disappeared
   while ordered queue rebuilds were in flight. A batch is not complete until
   Yahoo is read again and the final survivor order is confirmed.
4. **A snake-turn plan must be reset after pick one.** After selecting a QB,
   another QB remained at the top of the queue. The second-pick queue must be
   made safe before explanation or comparison work begins.
5. **Live research belongs between turns.** Targeted official-team checks and
   the independent news scanner improved late-round risk labels. On-clock
   decisions were strongest when reduced to one recommendation and fallback.
6. **Endgame queue order is roster state.** With one skill slot, DEF, and K
   open, the safe sequence was skill, preferred defense, then kicker. Backups
   for a filled special-teams position must be removed before the next clock.
7. **Final rosters cannot reconstruct control provenance.** Yahoo confirmed
   the players but not who clicked each one. Future logs must label every pick
   immediately as assistant-confirmed, manager-reported, Yahoo queue/autodraft,
   keeper, or not directly observed.
